## 1. WHAT IS ULTRA-PROCESSED FOOD?

### The NOVA Classification System
- Developed by researchers at the **University of São Paulo, Brazil**, proposed in **2009**
- Divides all food into **4 groups** based on the extent and purpose of processing:

| Group | Description | Examples |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **1** | Unprocessed / minimally processed | Vegetables, fruit, eggs, meat, nuts |
| **2** | Processed culinary ingredients | Oils, butter, sugar, salt, honey, flour |
| **3** | Processed foods | Canned beans, salted nuts, smoked meat, artisan bread |
| **4** | Ultra-processed foods (UPF) | Soft drinks, packaged snacks, reconstituted meat, most cereals |

### The Kitchen Test (Van Tulleken's heuristic)
> *"If an ingredient on a food packet isn't one you would normally find in a home kitchen, it's a UPF."*
— Dr Chris van Tulleken

- UPF ingredients include: hydrolysed proteins, soya protein isolate, gluten, casein, whey protein, mechanically separated meat, flavours, flavour enhancers, colours, emulsifiers, emulsifying salts, sweeteners, thickeners, anti-foaming agents, bulking agents, carbonating agents, foaming agents, gelling agents, glazing agents

### The Design Intent
- Van Tulleken's central argument: UPF is *"specifically engineered to drive excess consumption"*
- Low-cost ingredients, processed to maximise profit and palatability, not nutrition
- UPF is not simply "unhealthy food" — it is an **entirely novel category of substance** introduced into the human diet since the 1980s

## 3. THE SCIENCE — HEALTH OUTCOMES

### The Landmark BMJ Umbrella Review (February 2024)
- **Sample size:** 9,888,373 people across multiple cohort and case-control studies
- **Method:** Systematic umbrella review of 45 unique pooled analyses
- **Result:** UPF exposure was directly associated with **32 out of 45 (71%) health parameters** tested

#### Class I Evidence (Convincing):
| Outcome | Risk Increase |
|---------|--------------|
| Cardiovascular disease mortality | **+50%** (RR 1.50, CI 1.37–1.63) |
| Type 2 diabetes (dose-response) | **+12% per serving** (RR 1.12, CI 1.11–1.13) |
| Anxiety outcomes | **+48%** (OR 1.48, CI 1.37–1.59) |
| Combined common mental disorders | **+53%** (OR 1.53, CI 1.43–1.63) |

#### Class II Evidence (Highly Suggestive):
| Outcome | Risk Increase |
|---------|--------------|
| All-cause mortality | **+21%** (RR 1.21, CI 1.15–1.27) |
| Heart disease mortality | **+66%** (HR 1.66, CI 1.51–1.84) |
| Type 2 diabetes (prevalent) | **+40%** (OR 1.40, CI 1.23–1.59) |
| Depression | **+22%** (HR 1.22, CI 1.16–1.28) |
| Sleep problems | **+41%** (OR 1.41, CI 1.24–1.61) |
| Wheezing / respiratory | **+40%** (RR 1.40, CI 1.27–1.55) |
| Obesity | **+55%** (OR 1.55, CI 1.36–1.77) |

### Additional Research Findings
- **Colorectal cancer:** +4% per 10% more UPF consumed (CI 1.01–1.07) — *2024 systematic review*
- **14% of early deaths** in the UK attributable to UPF intake — *multi-country mortality study*
- **Inflammatory bowel disease**, metabolic syndrome, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease: similar associations, lower certainty of evidence

## 4. THE ADDICTION ARGUMENT

### Van Tulleken's Core Thesis
- UPF is not a matter of willpower or personal choice — it is a **physiologically addictive substance**
- The book reframes the obesity crisis from a failure of personal responsibility to a consequence of industrial food design

### The Science of UPF Addiction
- **2025 Nature Medicine paper** synthesised evidence from ~300 studies across 36 countries
- Ultra-processed foods can **hijack the brain's reward system**, triggering:
  - Cravings
  - Loss of control
  - Persistent use despite harmful consequences
- These are the **same clinical criteria** used to diagnose substance-use disorders (DSM-5)

### Global Prevalence of UPF Addiction
- **14% of adults** worldwide meet criteria for UPF addiction
- **15% of youths** meet criteria for UPF addiction
- *(Source: PubMed 2024, Curr Obes Rep.)*

### Biological Mechanisms
- Altered functioning across the **brain-gut-microbiome axis**
- Early evidence for **UPF withdrawal symptoms** when consumption is reduced
- Poorer **weight management outcomes** among persons with UPF addiction
- People of colour and those with **food insecurity** face higher health disparities from UPF addiction

### Van Tulleken's Self-Experiment
- Ate an **80% UPF diet for one month**
- Documented: weight gain, altered metabolism, changed appetite signals, psychological dependency symptoms
- "Ultra-processed products are food that **lies to us**" — on satiation signals being overridden

### The Dopamine Pathway
- UPF is engineered with precise combinations of fat, sugar, salt, and texture designed to trigger maximum dopamine release
- This is sometimes called the **"bliss point"** — a term coined by food scientist Howard Moskowitz
- Van Tulleken argues UPF causes greater weight gain than sugar or lack of exercise alone

## 5. HOW UPF HARMS THE BODY — MECHANISMS

### Disrupting Satiation
- UPF overrides the body's natural fullness signals
- Soft textures, engineered flavour, rapid digestion mean satiation hormones (GLP-1, PYY) don't fire correctly
- The body doesn't register that it has eaten sufficient calories

### The Gut Microbiome
- Emulsifiers (e.g., carboxymethylcellulose, polysorbate 80) used in UPF have been shown to damage the gut lining and alter microbiome composition in animal studies
- Disrupted microbiome associated with inflammation, metabolic disorder, depression

### Metabolic Disruption
- UPF consumption is strongly linked to insulin resistance
- Ultra-processing removes fibre, alters protein structure, destroys food matrix — all of which normally slow digestion and nutrient absorption

### Artificial Sweeteners
- Diet drinks do not resolve health risks
- Artificial sweeteners alter gut bacteria, affect insulin response, and may increase appetite
- The "zero-sugar" label is specifically called out as misleading in the book

## 6. THE INDUSTRY — HOW WE GOT HERE

### The Financialisation of Food
- Van Tulleken's key structural argument: the food system is **financialised**
- Publicly traded food corporations are legally obligated to maximise shareholder returns — not to produce healthy food
- This creates systemic, structural pressure to engineer addictive, cheap, high-margin products

### GRAS — The Regulatory Failure (USA)
- US food additives are regulated under the **GRAS system** (Generally Recognised As Safe)
- Companies can **self-certify** that new ingredients are safe without independent FDA review
- Thousands of additives have been introduced into the food supply with no mandatory safety testing
- FDA does not know about many GRAS self-certifications

### Marketing & Children
- UPF is disproportionately marketed to children and lower-income consumers
- 85.9% of global UPF regulatory interventions focus on changing the food *environment* (e.g., nutrition labelling) rather than restricting production or marketing *(Nat Food, 2025)*
- 47.1% of measures use only "consensus" to shape food company conduct — non-binding

### Historical Timeline
- **1960s–70s:** Food industry begins systematic investment in shelf-stable, highly palatable products
- **1980s:** UPF becomes dominant in Western diets; obesity rates begin rising sharply
- **2009:** NOVA classification proposed — first scientific framework to categorise UPF
- **2020:** Robert Lustig publishes *"Ultraprocessed Food: Addictive, Toxic, and Ready for Regulation"*
- **2023:** Van Tulleken publishes *Ultra-Processed People*; book becomes international bestseller
- **2024 (Feb):** Landmark BMJ umbrella review published — 9.9M people, 32 outcomes linked
- **2025:** The Lancet three-paper Series on UPF published; FDA + NIH launch joint initiative modelled on Tobacco Regulatory Science Program
- **2025 (Jul):** Nature Medicine paper synthesises 300 studies from 36 countries confirming addictive mechanisms

## 7. SOCIAL JUSTICE DIMENSION

- This is **not** primarily a problem of personal choice or willpower
- Lower-income households disproportionately rely on UPF due to cost, time, and access barriers
- UPF is contributing to **malnutrition** in sub-Saharan African nations as local food cultures are displaced
- People of colour face greater **health disparities** from UPF addiction
- The Middle East and North Africa have seen sharp increases in type 2 diabetes linked to UPF penetration
- Children and youth are specifically targeted by marketing

---

## 8. GLOBAL POLICY — WHERE THINGS STAND

### Regulation: Too Little, Too Narrow
- Only 417 UPF-related regulatory measures identified globally *(Nat Food, 2025)*
- 85.9% only address the food *environment* (labelling, location)
- Almost no measures target production, formulation, or corporate accountability

### What Experts Are Calling For (The Lancet, 2025)
1. Include UPF markers in **front-of-package labelling**
2. **Restrict marketing** of UPF to children
3. **Tax UPF** products and use revenue to fund access to healthy foods
4. Hold corporations accountable for promoting unhealthy diets
5. Incentivise food supply chains to shift toward minimally processed foods

### Regulatory Bright Spots
- **Brazil** was the first country to include UPF avoidance in national dietary guidelines (2014)
- **FDA + NIH joint initiative (2025)** modelled on the Tobacco Regulatory Science Program
- **WHO** launched a call for experts to develop a UPF guideline (July 2025)
